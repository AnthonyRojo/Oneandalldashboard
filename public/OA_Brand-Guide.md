## 1 · Brand Foundations

### 1.1 Brand Story
One&All Hub began with a simple observation: when school ends, the world often gets smaller for adults with disability — not bigger. We set out to change that. Today, in our thoughtfully curated home in Rosebery, we run a dynamic day service and holistic workshop hub where adults of all abilities come to create, move, cook, perform, connect and build.

From visual arts and performing arts to wellbeing, mobility, life skills and social enterprise, every workshop is led by skilled facilitators and grounded in person-centred active support. We don't define people by a diagnosis. We don't separate care from creativity. We believe it takes all sorts to build a community — and that every individual has something singular and worthwhile to bring to it.

**Short version (social bio):** A holistic hub for mixed abilities in Rosebery. Workshops in arts, wellbeing, life skills and social enterprise. It takes all sorts.

### 1.2 Vision
A world where every adult, regardless of ability, has a place to belong, create and contribute.

### 1.3 Mission
We help adults with mixed abilities to thrive — through creative, holistic and skill-building workshops that nurture body, mind and spirit.

### 1.4 Brand Values
- **Inclusivity** — We celebrate difference. Everyone belongs here — no diagnostic boxes, no labels.
- **Empowerment** — We back people to make their own choices, set their own goals and grow at their own pace.
- **Authenticity** — Real workshops, real relationships, real outcomes. No tokenism, no stock-photo smiles.
- **Community** — We're built on connection — between participants, families, facilitators and partners.
- **Integrity** — We're transparent, accountable and respectful in everything we do.

### 1.5 Brand Promise
We deliver high-quality, inclusive programs that empower individuals of all abilities to lead fulfilling lives — by fostering personal development, creativity and community contribution.

### 1.6 Positioning Statement
For NDIS participants aged 18+ with mixed abilities (and the families, support coordinators and facilitators who walk with them), One&All Hub is a dynamic day service and holistic workshop hub that uniquely blends arts, wellbeing, life skills, mobility and social enterprise into a single, person-centred experience — because we don't put people in boxes based on disability. We're inclusion-led, not diagnosis-driven.

### 1.7 Strapline
**holistic hub for mixed abilities**

### 1.8 Who We Are / Who We Are Not
| We are… | We are not… |
|---|---|
| A vibrant community hub | A clinic or welfare service |
| Inclusion-led | Diagnosis-driven |

## 2 · Logo System

### 2.1 Logo Anatomy
The One&All logo is built from three components — the symbol (a hollow ring with a centred dot), the wordmark (one&all in a custom rounded sans-serif), and the strapline (holistic hub for mixed abilities). The intentional off-centre placement of the symbol above the ampersand is a defining feature.

**Important:** The person symbol is never used without the wordmark. The lockup is the brand.

### 2.2 Logo Variations
Two structural treatments — the **Open Lockup** (no surrounding circle) and the **Round Badge** (lockup inside a solid filled circle). Both are co-primary; choose by context.

### 2.3 Co-Primary Use Case Matrix
| Use case | Use this version |
|---|---|
| Website header, letterhead, formal documents, email signature, partner proposals, business cards | Open Lockup |
| Social media profile photo, app icon, favicon | Round Badge |
| Merchandise (tote, tee, sticker, pin, mug, lanyard) | Round Badge |
| Posters, flyers, event collateral, signage | Either — designer's choice |
| End-of-newsletter sign-off, document footer | Round Badge |
| Single-colour print (engraving, stitch) | Open Lockup, solid black or white |

**Decision rule:** If the layout is square or circular — Round Badge. If it is wide or rectangular — Open Lockup.

### 2.4 Approved Colour Treatments
Round Badge restricted to three treatments only:
- Black round badge with white lockup (most versatile)
- White round badge with black lockup (clean, minimal)
- Yellow round badge with black lockup (signature use, applied sparingly)

### 2.5 Clear Space
Clear space unit = height of the "o" in "one". Maintain minimum of this unit on all four sides.

### 2.6 Minimum Sizes
| Application | Open Lockup | Round Badge |
|---|---|---|
| Print | Min. wordmark width: 35 mm | Min. circle diameter: 20 mm |
| Digital | Min. wordmark width: 140 px | Min. circle diameter: 80 px |
| Favicon / very small | Use no-strapline file | Min. 32 px |

### 2.7 Do's and Don'ts
**Do:** Use supplied artwork files as-is · Maintain clear space · Use approved colour combinations · Keep the symbol's intentional off-centre alignment over the ampersand

**Don't:** Separate the symbol from the wordmark · Recreate or retype the wordmark · Change proportions · Stretch, squash, skew, or rotate · Apply drop shadows, gradients, glows, or effects · Recolour outside approved combinations · Place on busy backgrounds without a solid colour panel

## 4 · Typography

### 4.1 Primary Typeface — Fira Sans
- Designer: Carrois Apostrophe & Erik Spiekermann
- Licence: Open Font License (free, Google Fonts)
- Approved weights: Light (300), Regular (400), Medium (500), SemiBold (600), Bold (700)
- Avoid: Hair, Thin, UltraLight, ExtraLight (fail accessibility)

### 4.2 Digital Type Hierarchy
| Style | Font · Weight | Size | Line height |
|---|---|---|---|
| H1 — Display | Fira Sans Bold | 48–64 px | 1.1 |
| H2 — Heading | Fira Sans SemiBold | 32–40 px | 1.2 |
| H3 — Subhead | Fira Sans Medium | 24–28 px | 1.3 |
| H4 — Label | Fira Sans SemiBold | 14–16 px | 1.4 |
| Body | Fira Sans Regular | 16–18 px | 1.6 |
| Body Large | Fira Sans Regular | 20 px | 1.6 |
| Caption | Fira Sans Regular | 13–14 px | 1.5 |

**Digital minimum:** Body text never below 16 px. Buttons and form fields never below 16 px.

### 4.3 Setting Body Text
- Line length: 50–75 characters per line
- Alignment: Left-aligned only — never justified, never centred for body
- Italics: For quotes only. Never for emphasis (use SemiBold)
- Underline: Reserved for hyperlinks
- All-caps: Sparingly — labels and tags only

### 4.4 Editorial Companion — Source Serif 4
Used for body copy in Annual Reports, board papers, formal proposals, donor stewardship letters only. Fira Sans remains the heading face.

## 5 · Brand Voice & Tone

### 5.1 The Four Voice Principles
1. **Warm, never sterile** — Write like a person who knows the participants, not a clinical case file.
2. **Empowering, never pitying** — "We back people to grow, create and lead." Not "We help those less fortunate."
3. **Person-first, never label-first** — "Adults with disability" not "Disabled adults"
4. **Plain, never jargony** — "Workshops to build skills and confidence." Not "Capacity-building therapeutic supports under SIL."

### 5.2 Tone by Context
| Context | Tone |
|---|---|
| Social — celebration posts | High energy, playful |
| Social — informational | Friendly, helpful |
| Website — homepage | Confident, warm |
| Website — programme pages | Specific, encouraging |
| Email to families | Personal, calm |
| Email to support coordinator | Warm + professional |
| Email to donor or partner | Confident, grounded |
| Printed handouts | Plain, direct, kind |
| Formal proposals | Clear, evidence-based, warm |
| Crisis or sensitive comms | Calm, accountable, brief |

**Test:** Read your draft aloud. If it sounds like a press release, too corporate. If it sounds like a Hallmark card, too sentimental. Aim for "knowledgeable friend."

### 5.3 Person-First Language Guide
**Use → Avoid:**
- people / adults with disability → the disabled, disabled people, handicapped
- person with a disability → disabled person, sufferer, victim
- participant → service user, client
- has [condition] / lives with [condition] → suffers from / afflicted by
- uses a wheelchair → wheelchair-bound, confined to a wheelchair
- person on the autism spectrum / autistic person → autistic (as noun)
- person with Down syndrome → Down's
- person with intellectual disability → mentally handicapped, retarded
- Deaf / hard of hearing → hearing impaired
- non-speaking / non-verbal → mute, dumb

**Note on autism:** Many autistic people prefer identity-first language ("autistic person"). If someone tells you their preference, use it. Person-first is the safe default for unfamiliar audiences.

### 5.4 Words Retired from One&All's Vocabulary
- Special needs → Disability / accessibility needs
- Differently abled → People with disability / mixed abilities
- High-functioning / low-functioning → Describe specific support needs
- Brave / inspirational (for routine activity) → Describe what the person actually did
- Wheelchair-bound → Wheelchair user
- Vulnerable → Specific terms (e.g. "people who need extra support")
- Sufferer → Person with / person who has
- Cared for / looked after → Supported / participates / attends

### 5.5 Key Messaging Framework
**Core message:** A dynamic day service and holistic workshop hub for adults of all abilities, in the heart of Rosebery.

**Three pillars:**
1. Workshops, not waiting rooms. Six programme areas — arts, wellbeing, life skills, mobility, performing arts and social enterprise — led by skilled facilitators.
2. Inclusion-led, not diagnosis-driven. We don't put people in boxes based on disability.
3. Real outcomes. Skills, friendships, confidence, and pathways into self-employment through our social enterprise programme.

## 6 · Imagery & Photography

Stock photography of people is **not permitted** in participant-facing content.

### 6.1 The Three Photo Tiers
| Tier | What it is | Use for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 — Hero | Professionally shot quarterly, consented, edited | Website hero, annual report, partner decks |
| 2 — Authentic in-the-moment | Phone/DSLR by staff during workshops, consented | Day-to-day social media, newsletters |
| 3 — Illustrative / non-people | The space, materials, food, artworks — no identifiable participants | When consent is missing, fillers |

**Monthly content ratio:** 70% Tier 2 · 20% Tier 1 · 10% Tier 3

### 6.2 Mood
Real · Warm · Active · Specific.

### 6.3 Consent — Non-Negotiable
No photo of a participant is published, printed, posted, or shared without **written, specific, current consent.**
- Written: signed media release form on file
- Specific: consent specifies channels and timeframe
- Current: reviewed annually
- Reversible: honoured within 7 days digital

### 6.4 Alt Text Standard
Formula: [Who] · [doing what] · [where, briefly] · [emotional or contextual detail]
- Don't start with "Image of…" or "Photo of…"
- Be specific, not flowery
- Don't name participants in alt text

## 7 · Iconography & Graphic Elements

### 7.1 The Hub Mark
A hollow circle used as a graphic device — never a substitute for the logo.

### 7.2 Programme Icons
Phosphor Icons (regular weight) — outlined, not filled. 2px stroke at 24px. Rounded caps and joints.

| Programme | Icon | Colour |
|---|---|---|
| Wellbeing & Mindfulness | Leaf or lotus outline | Slate Teal |
| Mobility & Fitness | Walking figure or motion lines | Hub Cyan |
| Cooking & Life Skills | Cooking pot or fork-and-knife | Burnt Orange |
| Visual Arts & Crafts | Brush or paint palette | Hub Pink |
| Performing Arts & Expression | Microphone or stage curtain | Hub Maroon |
| Social Enterprise | Shopping bag or growing seedling | Hub Yellow |

### 7.3 The Six Layout Devices
1. **Programme Tag** — Rounded pill with programme icon + name. Background: programme primary colour.
2. **Eyebrow Rule** — Thin 2px horizontal line above section heading, 60–100px wide.
3. **Quote Box** — Soft Cream background with 4px Hub Pink left border. Italic Fira Sans Light for quote.
4. **Numbered Step** — Hub Mark circle with numeral inside, 32–48px.
5. **Arc / Half-Circle** — Large coloured arc in one corner. Never overlap a participant's face.
6. **Three-Mark Divider** — Three Hub Marks in a horizontal row to break sections.

## 11 · Accessibility Standards

### 11.1 Locked Standards Summary
- Web: WCAG 2.2 Level AA minimum
- Body text contrast: 4.5:1 minimum · Large text: 3:1 · UI elements: 3:1
- Body font digital: 16px minimum · Print general: 11pt · Participant-facing print: 14pt
- Easy-read: 14pt minimum, 1.6 line height, plain language
- Touch target: 44×44px minimum

### 11.2 Cognitive Accessibility
- Reading level: Year 9 (Australian) for participant-facing
- Sentence length: Average ≤20 words; cut anything over 25
- Voice: Active, not passive
- Numbers: Use numerals ("12" not "twelve")
- Dates: "17 June 2026" — never US format
- Times: 12-hour with am/pm — "3:30pm" not "15:30"

### 11.3 Sensory Accessibility
- No autoplay video with sound. Ever.
- No flashing animations (>3 flashes/second)
- Honour prefers-reduced-motion
- Matt finishes only on print materials

### 11.4 Quiet Mode — Sensory-Friendly Variant
For intake forms, sensory-friendly event posters, mental health-related materials:
- Palette: Soft Cream + Warm Charcoal + one pastel only — no primary brights
- Typography: 14pt minimum, 1.6 line height
- Pattern: None — flat backgrounds only
- Imagery: Tier 3 only (hands and space, no faces)

### 11.5 Inclusive Design Checklist
Before publishing anything:
- Body text contrast ≥ 4.5:1
- Body text size ≥ 16px digital / 11pt print (14pt for participants)
- Left-aligned, not justified
- Real photos with consent (or Tier 3 hands/space)
- Alt text on every meaningful image
- No information communicated by colour alone
- Person-first language throughout
- Sensory check — no autoplay, no flashing
- AoC included on formal/external materials
